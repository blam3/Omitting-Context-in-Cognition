\begin{abstract}
Computational cognitive models are often compared using predictive fit or Bayesian evidence. We develop a constructive argument that shared omitted context can make a false model with additional utility curvature approximate the observed participant response distribution better than a linear context-omitting model. The current proof draft establishes strict joint Kullback--Leibler separation and separate asymptotic BIC, Bayes-factor, and leave-one-participant-out results for a specified two-trial setting. Both candidate joint distributions remain false. The result concerns mechanistic interpretation under misspecification, not superiority over a correctly specified contextual distribution. Extension to structural ambiguity models and empirical validation remain planned work.
\end{abstract}
