{\Large\bfseries\color{navy} Omitted Context, Observable Mixtures,\\ and Systematic Selection of False Complexity}\par
\vspace{6pt}
{\large Corrected theorem and streamlined proof}\par
\vspace{4pt}
{\small Revision of \emph{Central theorem, end to end: from omitted context to systematic false-complex selection} (2 October 2026)}
\end{center}
\vspace{5pt}
\begin{abstract}
\noindent We prove first, by an exact two-trial construction, that omitted context can make a homogeneous complex cognitive model strictly closer in joint Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence to the observed data than a nested homogeneous simple model, even though both models are false. We then give a short general sufficient condition: a nonzero population extra-parameter score at the simple-model KL projection. With fixed numbers of trials and finite design support, BIC, Bayes factors and exact trial-level Bayesian leave-one-out (LOO) select the false complex model with probability tending to one. A separate local weighted-projection formula explains which design signatures generate the effect. The hierarchical extension is \emph{conditional}: positive joint and conditional KL advantages must be established independently, and the regular local projection calculation requires interior, identifiable observable-law geometry. Zero-variance random-effects boundaries are not covered by that calculation. Relative to the initial draft, this version makes the closure formulation part of the definition of the risks and corrects score-drift, tangent-absorption, moment, parity and context-comparator overstatements; it also incorporates the fixes of an independent audit (29 October 2026).
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and the three distinct claims}
Participants $i=1,\ldots,n$ are independent and identically distributed. A participant has a retained cell $W_i\in\mathcal W$ ($\mathcal W$ finite; $H(w)=\Pr(W=w)>0$) and a fixed $T\ge2$ binary-response vector $Y_i\in\{0,1\}^T$. The true conditional law $p(y\mid w)$ is positive in every cell. One motivating latent specification is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:latent}
\Theta=a_0+a_1Z+\gamma C+U,\qquad
\Pr(Y_t=1\mid\Theta=\theta,W=w,C)=\pi_t(\theta,w)=\sig\{\kappa_t(w)-\beta_t(w)\theta\},
\end{equation}
with conditionally independent trials given $(\Theta,W,C)$; $C$ is omitted from the candidate fits. We write $\sig(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}$. The distribution of $\Theta\mid W=w$ is $F_w$, which can depend on $U$ as well as $C$.

A candidate $M$ is a set $\mathcal Q_M$ of conditional participant laws $q(\cdot\mid w)$, and $\mathcal Q_S\subseteq\mathcal Q_K$. The candidate families may be nonclosed; their closures are taken in the finite product of conditional probability simplices. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risks}
R_M=\min_{q\in\overline{\mathcal Q_M}}\KL_H(p\Vert q),\quad
\KL_H(p\Vert q)=\sum_wH(w)\KL\{p(\cdot\mid w)\Vert q(\cdot\mid w)\},\quad
\Delta_J=R_S-R_K.
\end{equation}
The minimum exists in the closure and is finite provided the family contains a positive law. As $p$ has positive cells, every minimizing law $q_M^\star$ is positive. $R_M=0$ \emph{if and only if} $p\in\overline{\mathcal Q_M}$, not necessarily $p\in\mathcal Q_M$. Let $q_M^\star$ denote a unique optimal \emph{law} when uniqueness is required; uniqueness of a parameterization is not assumed.

For the conditional target evaluated at the \emph{joint} KL projections, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalrisk}
C_M=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\E_{W,Y_{-t}}\KL\left\{p(Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W)\,\middle\Vert\,q_M^\star(Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W)\right\},
\qquad\Delta_C=C_S-C_K.
\end{equation}
This is exact \emph{single-response} prediction: all other trials of the held-out participant remain observed. The joint projection is not refitted to minimize $C_M$. If the optimal law is not unique, $C_M$ refers to a chosen optimal law; whenever $C_M$ is used for LOO, uniqueness is assumed (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}).

\paragraph{Selection criteria.} The data are $n$ independent participant records $D=\{(W_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$; $n$ counts participants, not trials. Each model is given by a measurable parameterization $\phi\mapsto q_M(\cdot\mid w;\phi)\in\mathcal Q_M$ with a fixed number $k_M$ of free parameters and a fixed proper prior $\Pi_M$. With $\ell_{M,n}(\phi)=\sum_i\log q_M(Y_i\mid W_i;\phi)$, define
\[
\mathrm{BIC}_M=-2\sup_\phi\ell_{M,n}(\phi)+k_M\log n,\qquad
m_M(D)=\int e^{\ell_{M,n}(\phi)}\,\Pi_M(d\phi),\qquad
\mathrm{BF}_{KS}=\frac{m_K(D)}{m_S(D)},
\]
\[
\mathrm{ELPD}_M=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{t=1}^T\log\widehat p_M(y_{it}\mid D_{-it}),\qquad
\mathrm{LOOIC}_M=-2\,\mathrm{ELPD}_M,
\]
where $D_{-it}$ deletes only the response $y_{it}$ and $\widehat p_M$ is the exact posterior predictive \eqref{eq:ratio}. The supremum is used because maxima need not be attained. BIC, the Bayes factor and LOO \emph{select} $K$ when $\mathrm{BIC}_K<\mathrm{BIC}_S$, $\mathrm{BF}_{KS}>1$ and $\mathrm{LOOIC}_K<\mathrm{LOOIC}_S$, respectively.

Three claims must be kept separate. (A) The exact witness and the homogeneous score-drift theorem are complete finite-support results. (B) The local weighted-projection formula is a regular, small-heterogeneity explanation, not a global equivalence. (C) Hierarchical selection requires distinct joint and conditional inequalities and, for the local formulas, additional geometric hypotheses.

\section{Elementary facts about omission and product models}
\begin{lemma}[Mixture, conditional moments and covariance]\label{lem:mixture}
Under \eqref{eq:latent},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
p(y\mid w)=\int\prod_{t=1}^T\pi_t(\theta,w)^{y_t}\{1-\pi_t(\theta,w)\}^{1-y_t}\,F_w(d\theta).
\end{equation}
If conditional second moments exist and $m_C=\E(C\mid W)$, $m_U=\E(U\mid W)$, then
\begin{align*}
\E(\Theta\mid W)&=a_0+a_1 Z+\gamma m_C+m_U,\\
\Var(\Theta\mid W)&=\gamma^2\Var(C\mid W)+\Var(U\mid W)+2\gamma\Cov(C,U\mid W).
\end{align*}
If $\Theta\mid W=w$ is nondegenerate and $\pi_t(\cdot,w)$ and $\pi_u(\cdot,w)$ are strictly monotone in the same direction, $\Cov(Y_t,Y_u\mid w)>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:mixture} is iterated expectation using conditional trial independence. The moment identities follow from linearity and the variance of $\gamma C+U$. For independent copies $\Theta,\Theta'$ from $F_w$, conditional trial independence gives
\[
\Cov(Y_t,Y_u\mid w)=\frac12\E\big[\{\pi_t(\Theta)-\pi_t(\Theta')\}\{\pi_u(\Theta)-\pi_u(\Theta')\}\mid w\big].
\]
The integrand is nonnegative and strictly positive with positive probability when both functions are strictly monotone in the same direction and $F_w$ is nondegenerate.
\end{proof}

Write $p_t(\cdot\mid w)$ for the true trial marginal and $I_w=\KL\{p(\cdot\mid w)\Vert\prod_t p_t(\cdot\mid w)\}$ (multi-information).
\begin{lemma}[Product reduction; the exact LOO identity]\label{lem:product}
For every positive product law $q(y\mid w)=\prod_tq_t(y_t\mid w)$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:productKL}
\KL_H(p\Vert q)&=\sum_wH(w)I_w+\sum_{w,t}H(w)\KL(p_t\Vert q_t),\\
\E_{W,Y_{-t}}\KL\{p(Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W)\Vert q_t(Y_t\mid W)\}
&=\IP(Y_t;Y_{-t}\mid W)+\sum_wH(w)\KL(p_t\Vert q_t).\label{eq:productC}
\end{align}
Consequently, for two product-model families, $\Delta_C=\Delta_J/T$ exactly. If the condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture} holds in a positive-probability cell, every product candidate satisfies $R_M\ge\sum_wH(w)I_w>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Insert the product $\prod_tp_t(y_t\mid w)$ between $p$ and $q$ inside $\log(p/q)$ and average. For \eqref{eq:productC}, insert $p_t$ between $p(Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W)$ and $q_t$. The dependence terms are independent of the candidate and cancel in $S-K$ differences. Positivity of covariance excludes a product truth, so the corresponding multi-information is positive. The inequalities extend to the closure because products form a closed subset of the finite simplex.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[What observing context buys]\label{lem:context}
For any context-ignoring law $q(y\mid w)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mutual}
\E_{W,C}\KL\{p(Y\mid W,C)\Vert q(Y\mid W)\}
=\IP(Y;C\mid W)+\KL_H(p\Vert q).
\end{equation}
Thus an exactly specified context-aware simple law has zero \emph{context-conditional} KL risk. A context-ignoring candidate has risk at least $\IP(Y;C\mid W)$. Its optimal context-conditional risk is $R_K+\IP(Y;C\mid W)$, which is positive if and only if $R_K>0$ or the law of $Y$ given $(W,C)$ varies with $C$ on a set of positive probability.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split $\log\{p(y\mid w,c)/q(y\mid w)\}$ into $\log\{p(y\mid w,c)/p(y\mid w)\}+\log\{p(y\mid w)/q(y\mid w)\}$ and average.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Context omission need not create nonzero variability: dependence between $C$ and $U$ can cancel its contribution in \eqref{eq:latent}. Conversely, heterogeneity in $U$ alone can create the same mixture effect. Neither the variance identity nor the mixture identity, by itself, implies an advantage for $K$.
\end{remark}

\section{The exact constructive theorem (the shortest proof)}
\begin{theorem}[A false complex model wins consistently: exact two-trial witness]\label{thm:witness}
Take $T=2$, $x_1=1$, $x_2=2$, $C\sim\Bern(1/2)$, $\Theta=C\log3$, and conditionally independent responses with $\Pr(Y_t=1\mid\Theta)=\sig(-x_t\Theta)$. Compare homogeneous models
\[
S:\quad q_t=\sig(-x_t\theta),\qquad
K:\quad q_t=\sig(\delta-x_t\theta),
\]
with $\theta\in[0,2]$ and $\delta=4\{w(1/2;\rho)-1/2\}$, $w(p;\rho)=\exp\{-(-\log p)^\rho\}$, $\rho\in[\rho_L,\rho_U]$, where $\rho_L=\log\{-\log(3/8)\}/\log\log2\simeq0.0528$ and $\rho_U=\log\{-\log(5/8)\}/\log\log2\simeq2.0600$ are the values giving $\delta=\mp1/2$ (the project's minimal contract, also used in its pilot simulations); $S$ is the restriction $\rho=1$ ($\delta=0$). Equip both models with proper priors having positive continuous density on their respective parameter boxes.

Then $0<R_K<R_S$, the unique optimal laws exist, and $\Delta_C=\Delta_J/2>0$. As the number of participants increases with $T=2$ fixed, BIC, the Bayes factor, and exact trial LOO all favor $K$ with probability tending to one, although $K$ is false. The extra parameter converges to $\rho_K^\star\ne1$ under the usual likelihood or posterior law-consistency statement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The true joint law, in the order $(00,10,01,11)$, is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactcells}
(p_{00},p_{10},p_{01},p_{11})=\frac1{80}(37,19,13,11).
\end{equation}
Thus $p_1=\Pr(Y_1=1)=3/8$, $p_2=\Pr(Y_2=1)=3/10$, and
\[
\Cov(Y_1,Y_2)=\frac{11}{80}-\frac38\frac3{10}=\frac1{40}>0.
\]
Every product model is therefore false and has risk at least $I(Y_1;Y_2)>0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:product}. Model $S$ cannot reproduce both trial marginals: matching them would demand
\[
\logit(3/10)=2\logit(3/8),\qquad\text{but}\qquad \log(3/7)\ne2\log(3/5).
\]
Model $K$ \emph{does} reproduce both marginals exactly with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactK}
\theta_K^\star=\log(7/5),\qquad \delta_K^\star=\log(21/25),\qquad \rho_K^\star\simeq0.662697.
\end{equation}
The corresponding $\rho$ lies in $(\rho_L,\rho_U)$ because $\delta_K^\star\in(-1/2,1/2)$ and the Prelec map $\rho\mapsto\delta$ is strictly increasing. By \eqref{eq:productKL},
\[
R_K=I(Y_1;Y_2)>0,\qquad
R_S=I(Y_1;Y_2)+\min_\theta\sum_{t=1}^2\KL\{\Bern(p_t)\Vert\Bern(\sig(-x_t\theta))\}>R_K.
\]
The strict minimum inequality follows by compactness and the impossibility of matching both marginals. Logit identifiability of the two distinct $x_t$ gives a unique $K$ law and unique $\delta$ and $\theta$; strict convexity gives a unique simple projection. Lemma~\ref{lem:product} yields $\Delta_C=\Delta_J/2$. The selection statement now follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} below and prior support. Since the nested submodel has a larger risk, every optimal $K$ law lies off $\delta=0$, and the identified, continuous inverse from law to $\rho$ gives parameter consistency.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the selection criteria converge}
\begin{theorem}[Finite-cell selection transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $\mathcal W$ and the participant response space be finite, let $p(y\mid w)>0$, and let each model contain a positive law. Suppose both model dimensions $k_M$ are fixed and their proper priors put positive mass on every law-neighborhood of an optimal law. Nesting is not required. Then
\begin{align}\label{eq:transfer}
\frac{\mathrm{BIC}_K-\mathrm{BIC}_S}{n}&\xrightarrow{p}-2\Delta_J,\\
\frac1n\log\mathrm{BF}_{KS}&\xrightarrow{p}\Delta_J.
\end{align}
If, in addition, each closure has a unique optimal law $q_M^\star$, then exact single-response Bayesian LOO satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loo}
\frac{\mathrm{LOOIC}_K-\mathrm{LOOIC}_S}{nT}\xrightarrow{p}-2\Delta_C.
\end{equation}
Hence if $\Delta_J>0$ and $\Delta_C>0$, all three criteria select $K$ with probability tending to one. If moreover $\mathcal Q_S\subseteq\mathcal Q_K$, the likelihood-ratio statistic for the nested restriction, $2(\sup\ell_{K,n}-\sup\ell_{S,n})$, diverges as $2n\Delta_J+o_p(n)$; any fixed critical-value test rejects with probability tending to one. This does \emph{not} assert a classical $\chi^2$ null law under misspecification or irregularity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We outline the argument; Appendix~\ref{app:transfer} gives complete proofs (Lemmas~\ref{lem:smallcell}--\ref{lem:loolimit}). Write $j=(w,y)$, $r_j=H(w)p(y\mid w)>0$, and $\hat r_j$ for empirical participant-cell frequencies. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the $N$ cells give $\Pr(\max_j|\hat r_j-r_j|>u)\le2Ne^{-2nu^2}$. Define $\ell(q)=\sum_jr_j\log q(y\mid w)$ and $\hat\ell_n(q)=\sum_j\hat r_j\log q(y\mid w)$. Convergence of $\hat\ell_n$ to $\ell$ is uniform on laws whose cells are at least a fixed $a>0$, and laws with a smaller cell are excluded from near-maximizers (Lemma~\ref{lem:smallcell}). It follows that
\[
\sup_{q\in\mathcal Q_M}\hat\ell_n(q)\xrightarrow{p}\ell_M^\star
=-R_M-\operatorname{Ent}_p(Y\mid W),
\]
where $\operatorname{Ent}_p(Y\mid W)$ is the conditional entropy of $Y$ given $W$. The BIC limit follows because $(k_K-k_S)\log n/n\to0$. The evidence $m_M(D)$ is at most $\exp\{n\sup\hat\ell_n\}$. For every $\delta>0$, a positive-prior-mass neighborhood of an optimal positive law has population log likelihood at least $\ell_M^\star-\delta$ and empirical log likelihood uniformly close to it; restricting the evidence integral to that neighborhood gives $n^{-1}\log m_M(D)\to_p\ell_M^\star$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:evidence}). Subtraction yields the Bayes-factor limit.

For LOO, the exact predictive identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
\widehat p_M(y_{it}\mid D_{-it})
=\frac{m_M(D)}{m_M(D_{-it})}
=\int q_M(y_{it}\mid y_{i,-t},W_i;\phi)\,\Pi_M(d\phi\mid D_{-it}).
\end{equation}
The posterior is based on all full participant vectors except that $Y_{it}$ is removed, and it uses the \emph{marginal} law of the retained $Y_{i,-t}$. Because the optimal law is unique, there is a strict population likelihood gap outside each neighborhood of $q_M^\star$. A single empirical-cell frequency event controls \emph{all} participant-deleted likelihoods: deleting any participant changes each empirical frequency by at most $1/(n-1)$, uniformly in $i$. The likelihood gap therefore gives exponentially vanishing posterior mass outside that neighborhood, uniformly over participant deletions. Restoring the other $T-1$ responses of participant $i$ reweights its participant-deleted posterior by the retained-response marginal $q_{M,-t}(y_{i,-t}\mid W_i;\phi)\le1$. On a sufficiently small neighborhood of the positive optimal law, this reweighting has a uniform positive lower bound, so the conclusion is also uniform over single-response deletions (Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion}).

Consequently, by continuity of conditional probabilities on the positive simplex,
\[
\max_{i,t}\left|\widehat p_M(y_{it}\mid D_{-it})-q_M^\star(y_{it}\mid y_{i,-t},W_i)\right|\xrightarrow{p}0.
\]
The predicted probabilities are bounded away from zero with probability tending to one, so their logs converge uniformly. The participant-level law of large numbers applied to $T^{-1}\sum_t\log q_M^\star(Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W)$ produces the LOO limit, because the true conditional entropies cancel in the $K-S$ difference (Lemma~\ref{lem:loolimit}). A union bound gives simultaneous selection. Under nesting, the likelihood-ratio assertion follows from the same likelihood limit.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The proof works for hierarchical laws without treating a participant's trials as independent. It does not require a regular Laplace approximation to a marginal likelihood. The extra requirement for LOO is a \emph{positive conditional gap}, not merely nesting or a positive joint gap.
\end{remark}

\section{General homogeneous theorem: the score-drift shortcut}
A homogeneous model has $q_M(y\mid w;\phi)=\prod_t\Bern\{y_t;\sig(\eta_{Mt}(w;\phi))\}$. Suppose $K$ embeds $S$ at an extra parameter $\rho=\rho_0$ and $\phi_S^\star$ is an attained \emph{interior} simple-model optimum. Let
\[
e_t(w)=\left.\frac{\partial\eta_{Kt}(w;\phi_S^\star,\rho)}{\partial\rho}\right|_{\rho=\rho_0},\quad
\Lambda=\sum_{w,t}H(w)e_t(w)\{p_t(1\mid w)-q_{St}^\star(1\mid w)\}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Strict false-complex advantage from extra-score drift]\label{thm:drift}
Assume the homogeneous setting above, with a differentiable feasible path in $\rho$ through the simple optimum. If $\Lambda\ne0$ and a small move in the direction of $\operatorname{sign}(\Lambda)$ is permitted, then $\Delta_J>0$. If in addition the truth is a mixture \eqref{eq:mixture} with nondegenerate $F_w$ and two same-direction strictly monotone trial kernels in some positive-mass cell, then
\[
0<R_K<R_S,\qquad \Delta_C=\Delta_J/T>0.
\]
Under the support and unique-law conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, BIC, BF and exact trial LOO all select the false complex model with probability tending to one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The population derivative of $\KL_H(p\Vert q_K(\phi_S^\star,\rho))$ at $\rho_0$ is $-\Lambda$. A feasible sufficiently small move in the improving direction decreases KL strictly below $R_S$, so $R_K<R_S$. Mixture-induced covariance gives $R_K>0$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:mixture}--\ref{lem:product}; the product reduction gives $\Delta_C=\Delta_J/T$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:drift}
$\Lambda\ne0$ is a \emph{sufficient}, not necessary, condition for $\Delta_J>0$. A zero derivative does not preclude improvement through second-order or nonlocal parameter changes. For example, on the design of Theorem~\ref{thm:witness} the family $K':\ \logit q_t=-\rho^2-x_t\theta$ ($\rho\in\mathbb R$) embeds $S$ at $\rho=0$ with $\partial_\rho\eta=0$, so $\Lambda=0$; yet $K'$ contains every nonpositive offset, including $\delta_K^\star=\log(21/25)$, so $\Delta_J=6.985\times10^{-4}>0$. The same family illustrates the singular directions of Remark~\ref{rem:absorption}. Also, the displayed sum is a weighted score--residual inner product, not literally a covariance without centering or additional structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A useful global lower bound]\label{prop:bound}
Suppose the simple and complex logits are jointly affine in parameters $(\phi,\delta)$, the simple optimum is interior, and the nuisance information $G_{SS}=J_S^\top\Omega^\star J_S$ is positive definite, where $J_S$ is the Jacobian of the simple logits with respect to $\phi$, stacked over $(w,t)$, and $\Omega^\star=\operatorname{diag}\{H(w)q_{St}^\star(1-q_{St}^\star)\}$. Put $e=\partial_\delta\eta_K$ and let
\[
\Lambda_\delta=\sum_{w,t}H(w)e_t(w)\{p_t(1\mid w)-q_{St}^\star(1\mid w)\}
\]
be the drift in the $\delta$ coordinate (if $\delta=\delta(\rho)$ reparameterizes $\rho$ near $\rho_0$, then $\Lambda=\delta'(\rho_0)\Lambda_\delta$). Let $c=e-J_SG_{SS}^{-1}J_S^\top\Omega^\star e$ and $B=\tfrac14\sum_{w,t}H(w)c_{wt}^2>0$. If the affine path $(\phi_S^\star-sG_{SS}^{-1}J_S^\top\Omega^\star e,\delta_0+s)$ remains feasible for every $s$ between $0$ and $\Lambda_\delta/B$ (automatic for a convex parameter domain once the endpoint is feasible), then
\[
\Delta_J\ge \frac{\Lambda_\delta^2}{2B}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Along the path, the first KL derivative is $-\Lambda_\delta$ because the nuisance score vanishes at an interior optimum. The second derivative is $\sum_{w,t}H(w)\sig'(\eta_{wt}(s))c_{wt}^2\le B$. Taylor's inequality bounds the risk along the feasible segment above by $R_S-\Lambda_\delta s+Bs^2/2$; substitute $s=\Lambda_\delta/B$.
\end{proof}

\section{A readable local mechanism: weighted residual alignment}
Write the unobserved heterogeneity as $\Theta\mid w=\bar\theta(w)+\varepsilon V_w$, where $\E V_w=0$, $\Var(V_w)=v(w)$, and $\sup_w\E|V_w|^3<\infty$; all design quantities are fixed. Let $\pi_{t0}=\sig\{\kappa_t-\beta_t\bar\theta\}$, $\pi'_{t0}=\pi_{t0}(1-\pi_{t0})$ (the logistic derivative expressed through the probability) and $p_0(y\mid w)=\prod_t\Bern(y_t;\pi_{t0})$.
\begin{lemma}[Attenuation and induced dependence]\label{lem:expansion}
Uniformly on the finite $(w,y)$ space,
\begin{align}\label{eq:hexpand}
p_\varepsilon(y\mid w)&=p_0(y\mid w)\{1+\varepsilon^2(h_A+h_D)+O(\varepsilon^3)\},\\
h_A&=\sum_ta_t(w)(y_t-\pi_{t0}),\quad a_t(w)=\tfrac12v(w)\beta_t(w)^2(1-2\pi_{t0}),\label{eq:att}\\
h_D&=v(w)\sum_{t<u}\beta_t(w)\beta_u(w)(y_t-\pi_{t0})(y_u-\pi_{u0}).\label{eq:dep}
\end{align}
Under $p_0$, $h_A$ and $h_D$ are centered and orthogonal. In particular,
\[
\logit\Pr_\varepsilon(Y_t=1\mid w)=\logit\pi_{t0}+\varepsilon^2a_t(w)+O(\varepsilon^3),
\quad
\Cov_\varepsilon(Y_t,Y_u\mid w)=\varepsilon^2v\beta_t\beta_u\pi'_{t0}\pi'_{u0}+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
If $V_w$ is symmetric with uniformly bounded fourth moment, the $O(\varepsilon^3)$ remainders above improve to $O(\varepsilon^4)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $g_y(\theta)=\prod_t\Bern\{y_t;\sig(\kappa_t-\beta_t\theta)\}$, Taylor expansion and $\E V_w=0$ give $\E g_y(\bar\theta+\varepsilon V_w)=g_y(\bar\theta)+\frac12\varepsilon^2v(w)g_y''(\bar\theta)+O(\varepsilon^3)$; bounded logistic derivatives and the moment assumption make the remainder uniform. Use $g''/g=\sum_t\ell_t''+(\sum_t\ell_t')^2$, with $\ell_t'=-\beta_t(y_t-\pi_{t0})$ and $\ell_t''=-\beta_t^2\pi'_{t0}$. The binary identity $(y-\pi)^2-\pi(1-\pi)=(1-2\pi)(y-\pi)$ gives \eqref{eq:att}--\eqref{eq:dep}. Centering and orthogonality follow from independence of the centered coordinates under $p_0$. Marginalization and the derivative of the logit give the displayed probability statements. Symmetry removes the cubic moment.
\end{proof}

The point is not that attenuation must lie outside $S$: it may be absorbed by a free intercept or another nuisance parameter. Only the \emph{unabsorbed} component matters.

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous weighted-projection criterion]\label{thm:wls}
Suppose $p_0$ belongs to both homogeneous candidate families and their local logits have full-column-rank Jacobians $J_S$ and $J_K=[J_S,e]$ at $p_0$. Assume the regular-chart hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier} at $p_0$: embedded, locally isolated $C^2$ observable-law charts with $p_0$ interior and no other model-law branch approaching $p_0$ (automatically satisfied for full-rank affine-logit families at an interior parameter). Define
\[
\Omega=\operatorname{diag}\{H(w)\pi_{t0}(1-\pi_{t0})\},\qquad
M_M=I-J_M(J_M^\top\Omega J_M)^{-1}J_M^\top\Omega,
\quad \tilde e=M_Se.
\]
Then, with $a$ given by \eqref{eq:att},
\begin{align}\label{eq:localrisk}
R_M(\varepsilon)&=\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2}\left\{\norm{M_Ma}_\Omega^2+\norm{h_D}_{p_0}^2\right\}+o(\varepsilon^4),\\
\Delta_J(\varepsilon)&=\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2}\frac{\langle a,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega^2}{\langle\tilde e,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega}+o(\varepsilon^4),\label{eq:wls}
\end{align}
where $\norm{h_D}_{p_0}^2=\sum_wH(w)v(w)^2\sum_{t<u}\beta_t^2\beta_u^2\pi'_{t0}\pi'_{u0}$, and $\tilde e\ne0$ because $J_K$ has full column rank. If the charts are $C^3$ (for example, affine-logit families), both remainders are $O(\varepsilon^5)$. In particular, if $\langle a,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega\ne0$, then $\Delta_J>0$ for all sufficiently small nonzero $\varepsilon$ and the local relative gain is determined by that partial alignment. For all homogeneous models, $\Delta_C=\Delta_J/T$ exactly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:expansion} gives $p_\varepsilon=p_0\{1+\varepsilon^2h+O(\varepsilon^3)\}$ uniformly, with $h=h_A+h_D$ and $\E_0(h\mid W)=0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier} below therefore applies with $k=2$; its proof uses only finite-simplex compactness, the chart hypotheses and KL expansions, and does not depend on this section. It supplies the localization of the \emph{global} projections at $p_0$, their uniqueness, and $R_M(\varepsilon)=\frac12\varepsilon^4\norm{h-P_Mh}^2_{p_0}+o(\varepsilon^4)$. It remains to compute the projections. A homogeneous model's scores are $\sum_t(\partial\eta_{Mt}/\partial\phi_j)(y_t-\pi_{t0})$, so $\mathcal T_M=\{\sum_tc_t(w)(y_t-\pi_{t0}):c\in\operatorname{col}J_M\}$. Independence of the centered coordinates under $p_0$ gives $\langle\sum_tc_t(y_t-\pi_{t0}),\sum_tc'_t(y_t-\pi_{t0})\rangle_{p_0}=c^\top\Omega c'$, and $h_D$ is orthogonal to every first-order function. Hence $P_Mh=P_Mh_A$ is the first-order function with coefficient vector $(I-M_M)a$, so $h-P_Mh=\sum_t(M_Ma)_t(y_t-\pi_{t0})+h_D$ and $\norm{h-P_Mh}^2_{p_0}=\norm{M_Ma}^2_\Omega+\norm{h_D}^2_{p_0}$; the formula for $\norm{h_D}^2_{p_0}$ follows from independence. The weighted Frisch--Waugh--Lovell identity $\norm{M_Sa}^2_\Omega-\norm{M_Ka}^2_\Omega=\langle a,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega^2/\langle\tilde e,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega$ gives \eqref{eq:wls}. For $C^3$ charts the minimizing chart coordinate is a $C^2$ function of the law (implicit-function theorem in the law variable, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier}), so the fitted density ratio is $1+\varepsilon^2P_Mh+O(\varepsilon^3)$ and both remainders are $O(\varepsilon^5)$. The final identity is Lemma~\ref{lem:product}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Correct scope of absorption]\label{rem:absorption}
If $\tilde e=0$, the additional parameter has no \emph{first-order} observable direction at $p_0$ and formula \eqref{eq:wls} with division by $\langle\tilde e,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega$ is not applicable. Without further regularity, a locally redundant first derivative gives no reliable order-of-magnitude conclusion: a singular higher-order parameter direction can affect even the leading risk term. Under a regular, locally isolated equal-tangent-space setting, the regular projection theorem instead gives a vanishing leading-order gap. An \emph{exact} zero gap for every positive truth requires that $\overline{\mathcal Q_S}$ and $\overline{\mathcal Q_K}$ contain the same positive laws, not merely equality of Jacobian spans at one point. Likewise $\langle a,\tilde e\rangle_\Omega=0$ means only that the leading-order gain vanishes, not that $\Delta_J=0$ for all $\varepsilon$.
\end{remark}

\section{Hierarchical models: a separate conditional extension}
Hierarchical candidates are participant laws of the form $q_M(y\mid w;\phi)=\int\prod_t\Bern\{y_t;\pi_{Mt}(\vartheta,w;\phi)\}\,G_M(d\vartheta\mid w;\phi)$. Their trial predictions condition on the retained trials through the posterior distribution of the participant effect. The product reduction \emph{does not apply}.

\begin{theorem}[Hierarchical selection, conditional on two distinct gaps]\label{thm:hier}
Assume the finite-cell setting and prior support of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, and unique optimal laws for exact trial LOO. For any nested hierarchical pair, if
\[
\boxed{\quad R_S>R_K>0\quad\text{and}\quad C_S>C_K,\quad}
\]
then BIC, the Bayes factor and exact single-trial LOO all select the false hierarchical complex model with probability tending to one. Neither the joint gap nor the conditional gap follows solely from omitting $C$. A nonzero feasible extra-score drift is sufficient for $R_S>R_K$, but the other two inequalities require separate proof.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}. The requirement $R_K>0$ declares the selected model false. The drift implication is the same directional derivative argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:drift}, applied to the full participant likelihood rather than to product logits.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular local hierarchical criterion, not a zero-variance theorem]\label{thm:localhier}
Suppose $p_\varepsilon=p_0\{1+\varepsilon^k h+o(\varepsilon^k)\}$ uniformly on finite cells, where $p_0$ is positive, $\E_0(h\mid W)=0$, and $k\ge1$. Assume both observable-law closures near $p_0$ are locally isolated, embedded $C^2$ manifolds with $p_0$ an \emph{interior} point of each chart; their derivatives have full rank and tangent spaces $\mathcal T_S\subseteq\mathcal T_K$ in $L^2(Hp_0)$. Assume no other model-law branch approaches $p_0$ outside those local charts. Let $P_M$ denote orthogonal projection, $r_M=h-P_Mh$, $d=P_Kh-P_Sh$, and define the positive-semidefinite operator
\[
\mathcal A=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\{I-\E_0(\,\cdot\mid Y_{-t},W)\}.
\]
Then unique optimal laws exist for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ and
\begin{align*}
R_M(\varepsilon)&=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{2k}\norm{r_M}_{p_0}^2+o(\varepsilon^{2k}),\\
\Delta_J(\varepsilon)&=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{2k}\norm{d}_{p_0}^2+o(\varepsilon^{2k}),\\
\Delta_C(\varepsilon)&=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{2k}\big\{\langle d,\mathcal A d\rangle_{p_0}+2\langle r_K,\mathcal A d\rangle_{p_0}\big\}+o(\varepsilon^{2k}).
\end{align*}
Therefore $d\ne0$, $r_K\ne0$, and $L:=\langle d,\mathcal A d\rangle+2\langle r_K,\mathcal A d\rangle>0$ are sufficient for the three gaps of Theorem~\ref{thm:hier} for all small nonzero $\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite-simplex compactness and the fact that $p_0\in\mathcal Q_M$ force every global KL projection of $p_\varepsilon$ to converge to $p_0$ in law space. Local isolation places it inside the specified chart. With full-rank derivative, the KL Hessian at $p_0$ is positive definite in chart coordinates $\psi$, and by continuity $\psi\mapsto\KL_H(p\Vert q_M(\psi))$ is strictly convex on a fixed ball for every law $p$ near $p_0$, so the minimizer in the chart is unique. Because $p_\varepsilon$ need not be differentiable in $\varepsilon$, apply the implicit-function theorem with the \emph{law} $p$ as parameter: the stationarity equation $\nabla_\psi\KL_H(p\Vert q_M(\psi))=-\sum_{w,y}H(w)p(y\mid w)\nabla_\psi\log q_M(y\mid w;\psi)=0$ is linear in $p$ and $C^1$ in $\psi$ for a $C^2$ chart, so the minimizer $\psi_M(p)$ is $C^1$ in $p$ near $p_0$. Since $p_\varepsilon-p_0=O(\varepsilon^k)$, this and a second-order KL expansion give
\[
q_M^\star/p_0=1+\varepsilon^kP_Mh+o(\varepsilon^k),\qquad
\KL_H(p_\varepsilon\Vert q_M^\star)=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{2k}\norm{h-P_Mh}^2+o(\varepsilon^{2k}).
\]
Nested orthogonal projections imply $r_S=r_K+d$ and $\langle r_K,d\rangle=0$, yielding the joint gap. The KL chain rule for $Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W$ subtracts the corresponding $Y_{-t}$ marginal KL. Marginalizing a first-order density perturbation $f$ replaces it with $\E_0(f\mid Y_{-t},W)$, hence $C_M=\frac12\varepsilon^{2k}\langle r_M,\mathcal A r_M\rangle+o(\varepsilon^{2k})$. Substitute $r_S=r_K+d$. Positive coefficients yield the stated strict inequalities.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Crucial boundary exclusion]
A Gaussian random-effect variance constrained to $\sigma^2\ge0$ is at a parameter-space boundary when $\sigma^2=0$. Ordinary interior-manifold hypotheses may fail there; tangent cones or direct one-sided arguments are needed. Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier} does \emph{not} prove the desired effect for a free-variance hierarchical model expanded around zero variance. Moreover $L$ can be negative; $d\ne0$ alone guarantees only the \emph{joint} improvement.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Variance matching sharply limits the hierarchical gap]\label{prop:variance}
Assume $\Theta\mid W=w=\bar\theta(w)+\varepsilon V_w$, $\E V_w=0$, and $\Var(V_w)=v>0$ is constant in $w$. Suppose the hierarchical simple model can choose the correct means and a Gaussian shared effect with variance $\varepsilon^2v$, and the trial product kernel is logistic with fixed finite design. If $\sup_w\E|V_w|^3<\infty$, then $R_S(\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^6)$, $0\le\Delta_J\le R_S=O(\varepsilon^6)$ and $\Delta_C\le C_S\le R_S=O(\varepsilon^6)$. If each $V_w$ is symmetric and $\sup_w\E|V_w|^4<\infty$, these bounds improve to $O(\varepsilon^8)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand each positive participant-cell probability $\E g_y(\bar\theta+\varepsilon V_w)$ in $\varepsilon$. The Gaussian comparator matches the mean and variance terms through order $\varepsilon^2$, and bounded third absolute moments imply a uniform $O(\varepsilon^3)$ probability difference. Symmetry plus bounded fourth moments improve this difference to $O(\varepsilon^4)$. Since every baseline cell is strictly positive, KL between normalized nearby laws is quadratic in their probability difference, giving $O(\varepsilon^6)$ or $O(\varepsilon^8)$. The optimal simple risk cannot exceed the comparator risk, and nesting implies $0\le\Delta_J\le R_S$. Finally, by the chain rule $C_M=T^{-1}\sum_t\{\KL_H(p\Vert q_M^\star)-\KL_H(p_{-t}\Vert q^\star_{M,-t})\}\le R_M$, and $C_K\ge0$, so $\Delta_C\le C_S\le R_S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Corrected symmetry/parity obstruction]\label{prop:parity}
Let $Z\in\{-1,1\}$ be balanced, with a common trial design and an otherwise $Z$-symmetric \emph{interior positive-variance} baseline law. Suppose the true local perturbation is $\lambda h$ with $h(Z,y)=Zs_v(y)$, the simple tangent space $\mathcal T_S$ is invariant under the reflection $Z\mapsto-Z$, i.e.\ it is the direct sum of its even and odd parts (e.g., the scores for $\mu_0,v$ are even and that for $\mu_1$ is odd), and \emph{the additional complex-model score is $Z$-even at the baseline}. Under the regular-chart hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier}, $P_Kh=P_Sh$ and $\Delta_J(\lambda)=o(\lambda^2)$. If $h=Zs_v$ has a component orthogonal to the odd part of $\mathcal T_S$, then $R_S(\lambda)$ is positive to order $\lambda^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Balanced $Z$ makes the even and odd subspaces orthogonal in $L^2(Hp_0)$. The perturbation is odd and the added score is even. Because $\mathcal T_S$ is reflection-invariant, residualizing the added even score against $\mathcal T_S$ leaves an even function, which has zero inner product with the odd perturbation. Thus adding it does not enlarge the projection of $h$, and the first-order-in-$\lambda$ version of Theorem~\ref{thm:localhier} yields the claims.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
``Adds one global parameter'' is not a sufficient parity assumption. An extra global parameter entering as $v(Z)=v+\delta Z$ has an \emph{odd} score and may fit context-dependent variance exactly. That counterexample is excluded by the explicit even-score hypothesis above.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Joint KL improvement need not imply conditional improvement]\label{prop:reversal}
Even for nested, positive finite-dimensional participant-law families there can be $\Delta_J>0>\Delta_C$. For $T=2$, in the cell order $(y_1y_2)=(00,10,01,11)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}, take
\[
p=(.27,.33,.28,.12),\quad s=(.11,.19,.12,.58),\quad k=(.68,.06,.05,.21),
\]
and $\mathcal Q_S=\{s\}$, $\mathcal Q_K=\{s+\lambda(k-s):0\le\lambda\le1\}$. The unique joint projection onto $\mathcal Q_K$ has $\lambda^\star\simeq0.281900$, and
\[
\Delta_J\simeq0.098320>0,\qquad \Delta_C\simeq-0.052440<0.
\]
Thus neither nesting nor a joint likelihood advantage establishes an exact trial-LOO advantage for general dependent-response models.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All displayed cells are positive. The function $\lambda\mapsto\KL\{p\Vert s+\lambda(k-s)\}$ is strictly convex, with derivative $-\sum_jp_j(k_j-s_j)/(s_j+\lambda(k_j-s_j))$. This derivative has a unique zero in $(0,1)$, giving the stated $\lambda^\star$. Compute $C(q)=\KL(p\Vert q)-\frac12\{\KL(p_{-1}\Vert q_{-1})+\KL(p_{-2}\Vert q_{-2})\}$ for $q=s$ and $q=s+\lambda^\star(k-s)$ to obtain the displayed gaps. The comparison is an abstract nested law-space example, \emph{not} a constructed witness for a particular Gaussian cognitive random-effects pair.
\end{proof}

\section{Context-aware comparison and theorem status}
\begin{corollary}[Measured context removes the correctly modeled component]\label{cor:ctx}
Let $C$ have finite support and be observed; suppose the simple context-aware model $S_C$ contains the true law $p(Y\mid W,C)$, including any residual $U$ distribution required for correctness. Compare it with a context-ignoring $K$ evaluated on $(W,C)$, using the same participant-level likelihood and fixed-dimensional proper priors. If $R_K+\IP(Y;C\mid W)>0$, then the \emph{context-conditional} joint KL gap favoring $S_C$ is strictly positive. BIC and BF consequently favor $S_C$ with probability tending to one. If optimal laws are unique, exact single-trial LOO also favors $S_C$ with probability tending to one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The context-aware truth has risk zero. Identity \eqref{eq:mutual} makes the context-ignoring model's risk $R_K+\IP(Y;C\mid W)>0$. For any two positive joint laws, zero conditional KL for every $Y_t\mid Y_{-t},W,C$ implies equality of all full conditionals and hence equality of joint laws (ratios of their masses are constant along edges of the binary hypercube). Therefore a positive joint KL risk implies a strictly positive average conditional KL risk when comparing the true law to the optimal incorrect law. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, which does not require nesting, on the enlarged finite design $(W,C)$ with $S_C$ in the role of the preferred model.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The positive-gap assumption matters: if $K$ also represents the truth on $(W,C)$, an asymptotic $n$-order preference cannot be inferred from KL alone. Including $C$ also does not make the simple model correct if the remaining $U$ heterogeneity is not modeled adequately.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof details for Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}}\label{app:transfer}
Throughout, $j=(w,y)$ ranges over the $N=|\mathcal W|2^T$ cells, $r_j=H(w)p(y\mid w)$ and $r_{\min}=\min_jr_j>0$. A candidate law is identified with its vector $q=(q_j)$, $q_j=q(y\mid w)$, in the finite product $\mathcal S$ of conditional simplices. ``Positive prior mass on a law-neighborhood $U$'' means $\Pi_M\{\phi:q_M(\cdot;\phi)\in U\}>0$. For $u>0$ let $E_n(u)=\{\max_j|\hat r_j-r_j|\le u\}$; Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give $\Pr\{E_n(u)^c\}\le2Ne^{-2nu^2}$. For $a\in(0,1)$ let $B_a=\{q\in\mathcal S:\min_jq_j\ge a\}$. On $E_n(u)$, for every $q\in B_a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unifB}
|\hat\ell_n(q)-\ell(q)|\le\log(1/a)\sum_j|\hat r_j-r_j|\le Nu\log(1/a).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Small-cell exclusion and the likelihood limit]\label{lem:smallcell}
Fix $M$, an optimal law $q^\star_M\in\overline{\mathcal Q_M}$, and $a\in(0,\tfrac12\min_jq^\star_{M,j})$ with $\tfrac12r_{\min}\log a<\ell_M^\star-2$. Let $u_0=\min\{r_{\min}/2,\,1/(N\log(1/a))\}$. On $E_n(u)$ with $u\le u_0$:
(i) every $q\in\mathcal Q_M\setminus B_a$ has $\hat\ell_n(q)\le\ell_M^\star-2$;
(ii) $|\sup_{\mathcal Q_M}\hat\ell_n-\ell_M^\star|\le Nu\log(1/a)$.
Consequently $\sup_{\mathcal Q_M}\hat\ell_n\to_p\ell_M^\star$ and $(\mathrm{BIC}_K-\mathrm{BIC}_S)/n\to_p-2\Delta_J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $E_n(u)$ every $\hat r_j\ge r_{\min}/2$. If $\min_jq_j<a$, every term of $\hat\ell_n(q)$ is nonpositive and one is at most $\tfrac12r_{\min}\log a$, proving (i). Laws in $\mathcal Q_M$ near $q^\star_M$ have all cells at least $a$, and $\ell\le\ell_M^\star$ on $\mathcal Q_M$, so $\sup_{\mathcal Q_M\cap B_a}\ell=\ell_M^\star$ and \eqref{eq:unifB} gives $|\sup_{\mathcal Q_M\cap B_a}\hat\ell_n-\ell_M^\star|\le Nu\log(1/a)\le1$. By (i), laws outside $B_a$ do not affect the supremum, proving (ii). The limit follows because $\Pr\{E_n(u)\}\to1$ for each fixed $u$. Finally $(\mathrm{BIC}_K-\mathrm{BIC}_S)/n=-2(\sup_{\mathcal Q_K}\hat\ell_n-\sup_{\mathcal Q_S}\hat\ell_n)+(k_K-k_S)n^{-1}\log n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Evidence]\label{lem:evidence}
Under the prior-support condition, $n^{-1}\log m_M(D)\to_p\ell_M^\star$; hence $n^{-1}\log\mathrm{BF}_{KS}\to_p\Delta_J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\Pi_M$ is a probability measure, $m_M(D)\le\exp\{n\sup_{\mathcal Q_M}\hat\ell_n\}$. For $\delta\in(0,1)$, the set $U_\delta=\{q\in\mathcal S:\ell(q)>\ell_M^\star-\delta,\ \min_jq_j>a\}$ is open, because $\ell$ is continuous on positive laws, and contains $q^\star_M$; hence $\pi_\delta=\Pi_M\{\phi:q_M(\cdot;\phi)\in U_\delta\}>0$. On $E_n(u)$, \eqref{eq:unifB} gives $m_M(D)\ge\pi_\delta\exp\{n(\ell_M^\star-\delta-Nu\log(1/a))\}$. Given $\eta>0$, take $\delta=\eta/3$ and $u$ with $Nu\log(1/a)\le\eta/3$; for $n$ so large that $n^{-1}\log\pi_\delta\ge-\eta/3$, $n^{-1}\log m_M(D)\ge\ell_M^\star-\eta$ on $E_n(u)$. Combine with the upper bound and Lemma~\ref{lem:smallcell}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Posterior concentration, uniformly over deletions]\label{lem:deletion}
Suppose $\overline{\mathcal Q_M}$ has a unique optimal law $q^\star_M$, and let $U$ be an open neighborhood of it in $\mathcal S$. Then
\[
\max_{1\le i\le n}\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-i})\xrightarrow{p}0,\qquad
\max_{i,t}\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-it})\xrightarrow{p}0,
\]
where $D_{-i}$ deletes participant $i$ and $D_{-it}$ deletes only $y_{it}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Shrinking $U$ only strengthens the conclusion, so assume $U\subseteq B_{2a}$ with $a$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:smallcell}. The set $(\overline{\mathcal Q_M}\cap B_a)\setminus U$ is compact; $\ell$ is continuous on it and, by uniqueness, strictly below $\ell_M^\star$ there. Choose $g\in(0,1]$ with $\ell\le\ell_M^\star-g$ on that set, and let $V=U\cap\{q:\ell(q)>\ell_M^\star-g/4\}$, an open neighborhood of $q^\star_M$ with $\pi_V=\Pi_M\{\phi:q_M(\cdot;\phi)\in V\}>0$.

\emph{Participant deletion.} The frequencies without participant $i$ are $\hat r^{(-i)}=(n\hat r-\mathbf 1_{j_i})/(n-1)$, so $\max_{i,j}|\hat r^{(-i)}_j-\hat r_j|\le1/(n-1)$. On $E_n(u)$, every $\hat r^{(-i)}$ is therefore within $u'=u+1/(n-1)$ of $r$, simultaneously for all $i$. Take $u$ small and $n$ large so that $u'\le u_0$ and $Nu'\log(1/a)\le g/8$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:smallcell}(i) and \eqref{eq:unifB} to the deleted frequencies, the deleted log likelihood $(n-1)\hat\ell^{(-i)}_n(q)$ is at most $(n-1)(\ell_M^\star-7g/8)$ for $q\notin U$ and at least $(n-1)(\ell_M^\star-3g/8)$ on $V$. Hence, on the single event $E_n(u)$,
\[
\max_i\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-i})\le\pi_V^{-1}e^{-(n-1)g/2}.
\]
\emph{Response deletion.} $\Pi_M(d\phi\mid D_{-it})\propto q_{M,-t}(y_{i,-t}\mid W_i;\phi)\,\Pi_M(d\phi\mid D_{-i})$, where $q_{M,-t}$ is the marginal law of the retained responses. The tilt is at most one, and on $U\subseteq B_{2a}$ it is at least $2a$, since every marginal cell of a law in $B_{2a}$ is at least $2a$. Therefore
\[
\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-it})\le\frac{\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-i})}{2a\,\Pi_M(q\in U\mid D_{-i})},
\]
and the right side tends to zero uniformly in $(i,t)$ by the first part.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trial-LOO limit]\label{lem:loolimit}
If both models satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion}, then $(\mathrm{ELPD}_K-\mathrm{ELPD}_S)/(nT)\to_p\Delta_C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a positive law $q$ and a cell $(w,y)$ let $F_t(q;w,y)=q(y\mid w)/q_{-t}(y_{-t}\mid w)=q(y_t\mid y_{-t},w)\in(0,1]$. By \eqref{eq:ratio}, $\widehat p_M(y_{it}\mid D_{-it})=\int F_t(q_M(\cdot;\phi);W_i,Y_i)\,\Pi_M(d\phi\mid D_{-it})$. The finitely many maps $F_t(\cdot;w,y)$ are uniformly continuous on $B_a$. Let $q^\star_{\min}=\min_jq^\star_{M,j}$ and $\eta\in(0,q^\star_{\min}/4]$, and choose a neighborhood $U\subseteq B_{2a}$ of $q^\star_M$ on which $|F_t(q;w,y)-F_t(q^\star_M;w,y)|\le\eta$ for all $(t,w,y)$. Since $0\le F_t\le1$,
\[
\max_{i,t}\bigl|\widehat p_M(y_{it}\mid D_{-it})-F_t(q^\star_M;W_i,Y_i)\bigr|\le\eta+\max_{i,t}\Pi_M(q\notin U\mid D_{-it}),
\]
which is at most $2\eta$ with probability tending to one by Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion}. Because $F_t(q^\star_M;\cdot)\ge q^\star_{\min}$, all predictions then exceed $q^\star_{\min}/2$, where $\log$ is Lipschitz with constant $2/q^\star_{\min}$. Hence
\[
\frac{\mathrm{ELPD}_M}{nT}=\sum_j\hat r_j\,\mathcal H_M(j)+o_p(1),\qquad
\mathcal H_M(w,y)=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\log q^\star_M(y_t\mid y_{-t},w),
\]
and $\sum_j\hat r_j\mathcal H_M(j)\to_p\sum_jr_j\mathcal H_M(j)$ because $\mathcal H_M$ is bounded. Finally $\sum_jr_j\{\mathcal H_K(j)-\mathcal H_S(j)\}=C_S-C_K=\Delta_C$, since the true conditional log-probabilities cancel. The law of large numbers is applied to participant cells; no independence between a participant's trials is used.
\end{proof}
